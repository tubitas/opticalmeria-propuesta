Your eye test in Albox and Huércal-Overa

Wearing glasses just means your eye focuses slightly off target, and that can be put right. What matters is checking it in time.

An eye test once a year

The World Health Organization recommends having your eyes tested at least once a year, to keep your prescription right and to catch eye conditions while they are still easy to treat. Even more so if they run in your family.

The test is done by a qualified optometrist and it doesn’t hurt: you read letters, look at lights and we take a photo or two of your eye.

Common eye conditions, explained

Short-sightedness (myopia)

The image focuses in front of the retina, so distant things look blurred. People often squint to see better, and if it isn’t properly corrected it causes eye strain and headaches.

It’s corrected with concave lenses, thinner in the middle than at the edges. Through them things look slightly smaller.

Long-sightedness (hyperopia)

The image focuses behind the retina and close work is hard. With a low prescription, young eyes compensate by working harder, but the price is headaches, watery eyes and red eyes after reading.

It’s corrected with convex lenses, thicker in the middle.

Astigmatism

The cornea is slightly more curved in one direction than the other, so the image is distorted both far and near. The symptoms are similar to long-sightedness: headaches, tiredness and irritation.

It rarely comes alone: it usually goes with short- or long-sightedness.

Cataracts

They aren’t a focusing problem, but they change your prescription: they reduce sharpness and cause glare. They appear in about 65% of people over 70, earlier with a lot of sun or a family history.

At first a change of prescription makes up for them. Later on, surgery is needed.

What we do in the consulting room

  • Eye tests for distance and near, and how both eyes work together.
  • Retinal photography: a photo of the back of the eye to compare from year to year.
  • Varilux varifocals, measured with precision instruments. We have been a Varilux specialist centre since 2000.
  • Contact lenses and Ortho-K, which corrects short-sightedness while you sleep.
  • Low vision: magnifiers, filters and other aids for people who no longer see well with glasses.
